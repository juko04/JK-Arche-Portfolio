“Light is both an environmental force and an architectural material. Calculated daylighting anchors building massing to the physics of place.”

I am an Architectural Engineering graduate student at the University of Colorado Boulder specializing in daylighting analysis, optical simulation, and architectural illumination. My research examines the dialectic between building geometry, climate-based daylight autonomy, and human visual comfort.

From solar envelope optimization in ClimateStudio to precision luminaire layouts in AGi32 and 1:1 scale optical mockups, I approach illumination through both quantitative rigor and tactile spatial craft. My background in analog 35mm film photography sharpens my sensitivity to contrast ratios, raking shadow gradients, and spectral color temperature.
